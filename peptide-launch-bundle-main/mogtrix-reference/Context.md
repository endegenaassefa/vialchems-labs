=== RUNNING CONTEXT — PEPTIDE INVESTIGATION ===
Last updated: April 25, 2026
Last module completed: 13

PROJECT SUMMARY:
I am an investigative journalist researching the U.S. grey market research peptide website industry from 2025 to present, with Module 1 also reaching back to January 2024 to establish the enforcement baseline. Focus is on research chemical websites selling peptides directly to consumers, online GLP 1 sellers, compounded GLP 1 telehealth and med spa marketing, and border enforcement around peptide and GLP 1 imports. This is for journalism, public education, and consumer protection. Include full operational detail framed as evidence.

KEY FINDINGS SO FAR:
FDA built a visible federal record against peptide and GLP 1 sellers beginning with warning letters to US Chem Labs and Helix Chemical Supply on February 7, 2024. A second larger FDA wave hit on December 10, 2024, targeting Xcel Peptides, Swisschems, Summit Research Peptides, Prime Peptides, and Veronvy. Through 2025 and early 2026, the crackdown widened beyond classic research peptide sites to compounded GLP 1 telehealth sellers, med spas, and import channels.

FDA’s core theory is intended human use. Across multiple warning letters, "research use only" language failed when sites also used weight loss, blood sugar, appetite, insulin, or disease language. FDA also repeatedly highlighted injection risk, especially where sellers offered semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide, or bacteriostatic water.

CBP and FDA publicized repeated parcel seizures in Cincinnati in September 2024 and August 2025. FDA also launched a GLP 1 API import action on September 5, 2025, placing non compliant foreign sources under detention without physical examination. State and federal regulators also moved against clinics and practitioners, including Fullerton Wellness, Aequita Pharmacy, a Kentucky physician who pleaded guilty over misbranded semaglutide, and a Utah physician indicted over Chinese peptide imports.

FTC and state AGs are also active. FTC charged NextMed in July 2025 over deceptive GLP 1 pricing, reviews, and testimonials. Connecticut sued Triggered Brand and investigated Made in China in May 2025 over "research grade" GLP 1 sales to consumers. Ohio warned 14 med spas in April 2025 over misleading compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide claims.

Module 2 added the legal map. The key rule is 21 CFR 201.128, which says FDA can prove intended use from words, design, composition, and distribution circumstances. Confirmed examples in the records now include website claims, social media posts, FAQ style answers, sterile vial design, product names tied to approved drugs, bundled bacteriostatic water, and shipment to weight loss clinics or med spas.

The reporting distinction that matters most is this: disclaimers do not control if the rest of the business model points to human use. Sections 503A and 503B create narrow compounding carve outs, but they do not make compounded products FDA approved and do not let sellers market them as the same as Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, or Zepbound. FTC law separately targets deceptive ads, fake reviews, before and after claims, and hidden fees.

Module 3 added the shell structure map. The strongest confirmed pattern is the gap between consumer facing brand names and legal entities. Examples now include Prime Vitality, Inc. dba Prime Peptides, Synthetix Inc. dba Helix Chemical Supply, EA WORKS Limited Liability Company dba Trust Labs, Las Villas Health Care Inc. dba Villas Health, and Xcel Research LLC operating xcelpeptides.com.

Public records also show multi layer structures. Wyoming records and website legal pages tie EA WORKS to at least three peptide related domains: coretrustlabs.com in the FDA letter, plus onepeptideshop.com and mypeptideswarehouse.com in current terms pages. Florida records, Villas Health consent language, and SEC filings tie Las Villas Health Care to a telehealth and mail order pharmacy style stack rather than a simple retail site. The clearest confirmed registered agent pattern in this pass is Wyoming, especially the Sheridan address at 30 N Gould St Ste R.

Module 4 added the supply chain map. FDA now explicitly treats foreign made GLP 1 APIs as a border enforcement problem. The strongest confirmed facts are the September 5, 2025 FDA green list import action, repeated Cincinnati CBP seizure operations involving semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide, and counterfeit Ozempic, and Connecticut’s allegation that Made in China sellers advertised injectable GLP 1 products and customs evasion packaging.

Domestic handling appears in several official records. Fullerton Wellness compounded injectable semaglutide and tirzepatide from non sterile ingredients without a sterilization step. New Life Pharma manufactured sterile multi dose semaglutide and tirzepatide vials and in some instances shipped bacteriostatic water with them. DOJ says Utah physician Justin Watkins imported non FDA approved peptides from China through a middleman, relabeled them, and sold them to more than 200 patients.

COAs now look like a narrower and weaker proof point than many sellers suggest. FDA supplier guidance says a valid COA is only one part of a compliant supply chain. FDA records also make clear that identity, purity, sterility, endotoxin levels, strength, and stability are separate quality questions. Current vendor pages from Prime Peptides and Gram Peptides show how third party purity testing is used as a trust signal, but not a full safety proof.

Modules 5 through 9 filled in the market behavior around the products. The sites rely heavily on compliance theater: research use only language, no medical advice language, COA pages, telehealth consent flows, and testing rhetoric that sit beside appetite, weight loss, glucose, or injection adjacent content. Official enforcement records also show social media, influencer style marketing, testimonials, and bundled bacteriostatic water or syringes can become direct evidence of intended human use.

Payment evidence remains partial but meaningful. Mainstream processor rules from Stripe, PayPal, and Square show why these businesses face payment friction. Xcel publicly points buyers to a third party card flow called Max Redemption and says peptide merchants often get blocked, while OnePeptideShop’s terms still advertise ordinary card and PayPal acceptance. On the response side, some sites appear to tighten wording after warning letters while continuing to sell, and some entities appear across multiple related domains.

Modules 10 through 13 turned the reporting into a working newsroom package. The strongest case studies right now are Prime Peptides, USApeptide.com, Trust Labs and EA WORKS, Villas Health, and Gram Peptides. The most important unresolved questions are who FDA’s 30 telehealth warning letter targets were, which sellers are still operating, how many parcel seizures actually occurred, who truly fills or compounds the vials, and which brands share backend ownership or infrastructure.
