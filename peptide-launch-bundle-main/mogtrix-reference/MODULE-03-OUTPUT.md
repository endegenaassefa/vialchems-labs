# MODULE 3: BUSINESS FORMATION AND SHELL STRUCTURE

## A. Narrative explanation of how these businesses are structured

The public record does not support one single business model. It supports a few recurring patterns.

The first pattern is a gap between the **brand the customer sees** and the **legal entity regulators cite**. FDA’s warning letters already show this clearly. Prime Peptides was cited as **Prime Vitality, Inc. dba Prime Peptides**. Helix Chemical Supply was cited as **Synthetix Inc. dba Helix Chemical Supply**. Trust Labs was cited as **EA WORKS Limited Liability Company dba Trust Labs**. Villas Health was cited as **Las Villas Health Care LLC dba Villas Health**. Xcel Peptides’ own terms page says the website is operated by **Xcel Research LLC** and that payments are made payable to that company. This matters because the customer often sees only the storefront brand, not the entity that may hold the merchant account, receive legal notices, or appear in a warning letter. [(Prime Peptides warning letter)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/prime-vitality-inc-dba-prime-peptides-695156-12102024) [(Helix warning letter)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/synthetix-inc-dba-helix-chemical-supply-668918-02072024) [(Trust Labs warning letter)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/ea-works-limited-liability-company-dba-trust-labs-715798-09092025) [(Villas Health warning letter)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/las-villas-health-care-llc-dba-villas-health-09092025) [(Xcel terms page)](https://www.xcelpeptides.com/terms-of-service/)

The second pattern is **heavy use of registered agent or mailbox style addresses**. The clearest example in this pass is EA WORKS in Wyoming. FDA’s September 9, 2025 warning letter to Trust Labs lists EA WORKS at **30 N Gould St Ste R, Sheridan, Wyoming**. A current Wyoming business search result for EA WORKS shows the same address and identifies **Registered Agents Inc.** as the registered agent. Wyoming’s Secretary of State also explains why these addresses recur. The state says a commercial registered agent can represent more than ten businesses and that some commercial registered agents represent hundreds or thousands of businesses at one address. Wyoming also says the registered office must be a physical Wyoming address, while the natural person contact can remain with the registered agent unless the entity chooses to make that information public. In plain English, that means a public filing can show the registered agent address without naming the operator the public would most want to identify. [(Trust Labs warning letter)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/ea-works-limited-liability-company-dba-trust-labs-715798-09092025) [(Wyoming EA WORKS business search result)](https://wyobiz.wyo.gov/Business/FilingDetails.aspx?eFNum=040026109250153238100098184023250152046009201137) [(Wyoming registered agent FAQ)](https://sos.wyo.gov/faqs.aspx?root=RAO) [(Wyoming commercial registered agent page)](https://sos.wyo.gov/business/commercialregagent.aspx)

The third pattern is **one legal entity tied to multiple customer facing domains**. Trust Labs is the cleanest example. FDA’s warning letter names the reviewed website as **coretrustlabs.com**. But current terms pages on **onepeptideshop.com** and **mypeptideswarehouse.com** both say they are operated by **EA WORKS Limited Liability Company** at the same Sheridan, Wyoming address. That does not prove illicit rebranding by itself. It does show that the same LLC has publicly connected itself to multiple peptide retail domains. For a reporter, that is exactly the kind of link that makes "separate" brands worth tracing together. [(Trust Labs warning letter)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/ea-works-limited-liability-company-dba-trust-labs-715798-09092025) [(OnePeptideShop terms)](https://onepeptideshop.com/terms-and-conditions/) [(My Peptides Warehouse terms)](https://mypeptideswarehouse.com/terms-conditions/)

The fourth pattern is **telehealth style layering rather than a pure research chemical storefront**. Villas Health shows that structure in public records. Florida’s Sunbiz record for **Las Villas Health Care Inc.** lists a Florida principal address, a registered agent, a filing date, a FEI/EIN number, and named managers or officers. Villas Health’s consent page says patients can obtain an online visit with an independent licensed professional and then opt into mail order pharmacy services. A separate SEC filing shows **Earth Science Tech, Inc.** completed the acquisition of Las Villas Health Care and DOConsultations in April 2025. Read together, those records suggest a stack that can include a clinic or telehealth front end, a prescribing layer, and a pharmacy or mail order fulfillment layer. That is not speculation. It is the picture those public records already draw. [(Florida Sunbiz detail for Las Villas Health Care Inc.)](https://search.sunbiz.org/Inquiry/CorporationSearch/SearchResultDetail?aggregateId=domp-p22000047538-72b12db5-527c-47a5-bc1d-7f2a2cd7c1cc&directionType=Initial&inquirytype=EntityName&listNameOrder=LASVILLASFAMILY+L150000129990&searchNameOrder=LASVILLASHEALTHCARE+P220000475380&searchTerm=LAS+VILLAS+FAMILY+LLC) [(Villas Health consent page)](https://villashealth.com/consent-to-telehealth/) [(SEC 8-K, April 1, 2025)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1538495/000164117225002889/form8-k.htm)

The fifth pattern is **keeping operational detail off the public filing while leaving clues on the website**. Florida’s Sunbiz record for Las Villas reveals an FEI/EIN number and named managers. Wyoming’s public materials show the registered agent system and filing history for EA WORKS. Xcel’s terms page shows that checkout payments are payable to Xcel Research LLC. These fragments matter because public records do not usually show a merchant processor, fulfillment contractor, overseas supplier, or bank account. Those links usually surface only in court files, patient billing records, import records, shipping labels, or leaked internal documents. In other words, the state filing alone rarely tells the whole story. It gives the first node. [(Florida Sunbiz detail)](https://search.sunbiz.org/Inquiry/CorporationSearch/SearchResultDetail?aggregateId=domp-p22000047538-72b12db5-527c-47a5-bc1d-7f2a2cd7c1cc&directionType=Initial&inquirytype=EntityName&listNameOrder=LASVILLASFAMILY+L150000129990&searchNameOrder=LASVILLASHEALTHCARE+P220000475380&searchTerm=LAS+VILLAS+FAMILY+LLC) [(Wyoming EA WORKS business search result)](https://wyobiz.wyo.gov/Business/FilingDetails.aspx?eFNum=040026109250153238100098184023250152046009201137) [(Xcel terms page)](https://www.xcelpeptides.com/terms-of-service/)

What about the states people always mention in LLC privacy discussions: **Wyoming, Delaware, New Mexico, and Nevada**. In this pass, I directly confirmed Wyoming and Florida in named peptide related examples, plus California and New York through FDA warning letter addresses. I did **not** directly confirm Delaware, New Mexico, or Nevada as formation states for the named peptide sellers in this module. I can still say two narrow things from official sources. Delaware requires every entity to appoint a registered agent and says the registered office "may, but need not be, the same as its place of business." Wyoming similarly requires a physical registered office and allows large commercial agents to represent many businesses. Those rules help explain why shell like structures are attractive there. But any broader claim that Delaware, New Mexico, or Nevada are the dominant states in this peptide niche should be treated as an **INVESTIGATIVE HYPOTHESIS** until it is backed by a larger entity level sample. [(Delaware registered agent FAQ)](https://corp.delaware.gov/faqs-regarding-registered-agents/) [(Wyoming registered agent FAQ)](https://sos.wyo.gov/faqs.aspx?root=RAO) [(Wyoming commercial registered agent page)](https://sos.wyo.gov/business/commercialregagent.aspx)

On web infrastructure, the public record shows at least some operators using mainstream ecommerce and telehealth stacks, not bespoke laboratory systems. Current HTML and legal pages show Xcel operating a conventional online storefront with customer checkout language. Current RDAP data for **xcelpeptides.com** lists Cloudflare name servers, which is one small but concrete sign of mainstream shielding and caching infrastructure. Villas Health uses a conventional patient consent flow on its website. **INVESTIGATIVE HYPOTHESIS:** across this market, operators likely prefer off the shelf commerce systems, common DNS intermediaries, and quickly replaceable domains because those are easier to launch, copy, and swap than custom platforms. This needs broader technical sampling before it should be written as a confirmed market wide fact. [(Xcel terms page)](https://www.xcelpeptides.com/terms-of-service/) [(RDAP record for xcelpeptides.com)](https://rdap.org/domain/xcelpeptides.com) [(Villas Health consent page)](https://villashealth.com/consent-to-telehealth/)

The main reporting lesson is simple. A peptide site is usually not just a website. It is a chain of public and semi public identities: domain, brand name, LLC or corporation, registered agent address, customer support email, payment payee, and sometimes a separate telehealth or pharmacy layer. The point of the shell structure is not to make the business invisible. The point is to make the links slow and tedious enough that a casual customer or a rushed regulator may stop after the first layer. That final sentence is an **INVESTIGATIVE INTERPRETATION** based on the documented structures above.

## B. Diagram description of a typical shell structure

1. A customer sees a consumer brand and domain such as a peptide store or weight loss site.
2. The site’s footer, terms page, or FDA warning letter points to a different legal entity name.
3. The legal entity is filed in a business friendly state or in the operating state, often using a registered agent or business service address.
4. Customer support uses a brand email address, which may not match the LLC name.
5. Payments may be payable to the legal entity named in the terms page or to a related entity. This step is confirmed for Xcel Research LLC and remains an investigative question for other brands.
6. In telehealth cases, a separate clinical entity or platform handles visits and prescription flow.
7. A pharmacy, outsourcing facility, or fulfillment layer may sit behind that telehealth front end.
8. If enforcement hits one layer, the public facing domain or brand can potentially change faster than the underlying business relationships.

## C. Checklist of public records a journalist should pull for each company

1. FDA warning letter, press release, court filing, or agency complaint naming the company
2. State business registry record for every named entity and every DBA
3. Annual reports, reinstatement filings, and name change filings
4. Registered agent name and address
5. Principal office and mailing address
6. Officer, manager, or organizer names where public
7. FEI/EIN fields where the state publishes them
8. Current terms of service, privacy policy, checkout terms, and consent forms
9. Current domain RDAP or WHOIS record
10. Historical domain snapshots from the Wayback Machine
11. Current and archived social media account names tied to the brand
12. SEC filings if the entity or its parent is public
13. Trademark applications for brand names and logos
14. BBB, Trustpilot, and consumer complaint listings, used as leads not primary proof
15. Court dockets for contract disputes, collections, employment cases, or injunctions
16. Pharmacy board records if a clinical or compounding layer is involved
17. Shipping labels, invoices, or receipts from customers if available
18. Parcel seizure or import records tied to the named entity or address
