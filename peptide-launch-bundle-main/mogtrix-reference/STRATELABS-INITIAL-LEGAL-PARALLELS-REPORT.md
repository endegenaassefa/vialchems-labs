# STRATELABS.IS — INITIAL LEGAL PARALLELS REPORT

Prepared: April 27, 2026  
Status: Initial structural review for journalism and consumer-protection reporting

## Executive Takeaway

StrateLabs appears to use the same compliance-theater structure documented elsewhere in this reporting set: scientific or lab branding, research-use-only positioning, a consumer-facing e-commerce flow, heavy disclaimer language, buyer-side risk shifting, narrow refund protections, arbitration barriers, and broad seller discretion over transactions.

The strongest parallel is the gap between the site’s legal distancing language and the broader commercial presentation. That pattern does not by itself prove illegality, but it is highly relevant to the intended-use and disclaimer-failure framework developed in prior modules.

## Key Legal Clauses Found

### 1. All sales final

The return policy states: "All Sales are final."

Why it matters: This sharply limits post-purchase remedies and pushes more transaction risk onto the customer.

### 2. Damage claims require proof

The site says damaged products may be replaced if sufficient proof is provided.

Why it matters: The seller appears to retain discretion over what counts as adequate proof.

### 3. Narrow cancellation window

The site says cancellations must happen before shipment, and the FAQ says orders are held for roughly one hour for cancellation attempts.

Parallel: This matches the rapid-fulfillment model that reduces consumer reversal options.

### 4. Binding arbitration in Texas

The informed consent policy routes disputes to binding arbitration in Texas and includes waiver language for jury trial and class action rights.

Why it matters: This materially reduces access to ordinary court remedies.

### 5. Broad liability disclaimer

The site says products are sold "as-is" and disclaims liability for direct, indirect, incidental, and consequential damages.

Parallel: This matches the liability-shield pattern seen on other research-peptide storefronts.

### 6. Indemnity / hold harmless

The customer agrees to indemnify and hold the seller harmless for claims tied to misuse or third-party involvement.

Why it matters: This can push litigation and defense costs back onto the buyer.

### 7. Research use only / no human consumption position

The site says products are not for human consumption, animal consumption, medical or therapeutic use, diagnostic use, dietary supplements, pharmaceuticals, or recreational use.

Parallel: This is the same intended-use distancing language central to the broader reporting framework.

### 8. Seller can refuse orders

The company reserves the right to cancel any order, request additional documentation, and refuse sales by person, organization, or jurisdiction.

Why it matters: This gives the seller broad unilateral control over the transaction.

## Operational Parallels Beyond Legal Text

### Warehousing and international structure

The site claims headquarters in the Netherlands while also referencing fulfillment warehouses in Texas and Delaware.

Investigative angle: A cross-border entity paired with domestic fulfillment is worth mapping through entity records, warehouse addresses, payment descriptors, and shipping materials.

### Sterility and lab-confidence marketing

Product pages reportedly emphasize sterilized vials, tamper-proof seals, purity claims, public lab reports, and injectable-vial handling.

Why it matters: That language can create quasi-medical confidence signals even while the legal pages disclaim human use.
