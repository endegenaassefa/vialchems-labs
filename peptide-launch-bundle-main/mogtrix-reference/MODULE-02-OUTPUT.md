# MODULE 2: THE LEGAL GREY ZONE

This is a reporting memo, not legal advice.

## A. Plain English explanation of the grey zone

The grey market peptide business lives in a gap between what sellers say and what they actually do. On paper, many sites say their products are "for research use only" or "not for human consumption." In practice, FDA looks at the whole picture, not just one disclaimer. The core rule is FDA’s "intended use" rule at 21 CFR 201.128. That rule says intent can be shown by a seller’s words, the design or composition of the product, and the circumstances around how it is distributed. FDA gives examples such as labeling claims, advertising, oral statements, written statements, and surrounding circumstances. [(21 CFR 201.128)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title21-vol4/pdf/CFR-2025-title21-vol4-sec201-128.pdf)

That is why the "research use only" label often fails. FDA has already used this theory against peptide sellers. In February 2024, FDA told US Chem Labs and Helix Chemical Supply that semaglutide and tirzepatide were still intended as human drugs even though the sites used research only language, because the sites also made claims about weight loss, blood sugar, and disease. [(US Chem Labs warning letter)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/us-chem-labs-669074-02072024) [(Helix warning letter)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/synthetix-inc-dba-helix-chemical-supply-668918-02072024)

FDA does not need one magic sentence to prove intended use. It can build the case from many smaller clues. Confirmed examples in the records I reviewed include website claims, product names, social media posts, FAQ style answers, label text, design as sterile multi dose vials, bundled bacteriostatic water, and shipment to weight loss clinics or med spas. Prime Peptides shows FDA using Facebook, Instagram, and YouTube posts. NewSelf shows FDA using FAQ style claims such as "What is the FDA approved use of Wegovy?" Gram Peptides and New Life Pharma show FDA using bundled bacteriostatic water, sterile vial design, and distribution circumstances as intended use evidence. [(Prime Peptides warning letter)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/prime-vitality-inc-dba-prime-peptides-695156-12102024) [(NewSelf warning letter)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/newself-limited-dba-newself-721472-02202026) [(Gram Peptides warning letter)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/gram-peptides-721806-03312026) [(New Life Pharma warning letter)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/new-life-pharma-llc-725661-04142026)

Based on that rule, other marketing tools can also matter when the seller uses them or adopts them. That includes checkout language, email marketing, affiliate content, testimonials, customer reviews shown in ads, before and after photos, and influencer posts. FDA’s regulation covers written statements and surrounding circumstances. FTC guidance separately says health product ads can make both express and implied claims, and those claims have to be truthful, not misleading, and backed by competent and reliable scientific evidence. FTC’s NextMed case shows how fake testimonials, before and after photos, hidden terms, and manipulated reviews become part of an enforcement case. [(21 CFR 201.128)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title21-vol4/pdf/CFR-2025-title21-vol4-sec201-128.pdf) [(FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance)](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance) [(FTC NextMed release)](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/07/ftc-takes-action-against-telemedicine-firm-nextmed-over-charges-it-used-misleading-prices-fake)

The Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act matters most because it lets FDA treat these products as drugs when they are sold for disease treatment, weight loss, blood sugar control, or body changing effects. Once a product is treated as a drug, two big questions follow. First, is it an approved drug. Second, is it properly labeled. If the answer to either is no, FDA can call it an unapproved new drug, a misbranded drug, or both. That is the pattern across the warning letters in Module 1. [(FDA Roundup, February 13, 2024)](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-roundup-february-13-2024) [(FDA Roundup, December 17, 2024)](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-roundup-december-17-2024)

Compounding adds another layer. Compounded drugs are not FDA approved. FDA says they may be appropriate only when a patient’s medical need cannot be met by an FDA approved drug, or when the approved drug is not commercially available. FDA also says compounded drugs are not the same as generic drugs. Sections 503A and 503B create limited carve outs from some FDA requirements, but only when strict conditions are met. Section 503A is for patient specific compounding by a licensed pharmacist or physician. Section 503B is for registered outsourcing facilities. Both sections limit what bulk substances can be used, and 503A also bars regular compounding of products that are essentially copies of commercially available drugs. [(FDA concerns with unapproved GLP-1 drugs)](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/postmarket-drug-safety-information-patients-and-providers/medications-containing-semaglutide-marketed-type-2-diabetes-or-weight-loss?os=av) [(FDA section 503A page)](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/section-503a-federal-food-drug-and-cosmetic-act) [(FDA bulk substances page)](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/bulk-drug-substances-used-compounding) [(FDA 503A copy guidance)](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/compounded-drug-products-are-essentially-copies-commercially-available-drug-product-under-section)

That is why sellers get into trouble when they market compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide as the same thing as Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, or Zepbound. In September 2025 and February 2026, FDA sent warning letters saying that "same active ingredient" claims and branding that implied the site itself was the compounder were false or misleading. FDA also said compounded products are not FDA approved and are not the same as generics. [(FDA March 3, 2026 GLP-1 warning letter release)](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-warns-30-telehealth-companies-against-illegal-marketing-compounded-glp-1s) [(BluefitMD warning letter)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/bluefitmd-721446-02202026) [(NewSelf warning letter)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/newself-limited-dba-newself-721472-02202026)

Import law also matters because many of these products or their ingredients come from outside the regulated US supply chain. FDA says imported products must meet the same standards as domestic ones. FDA may refuse products that violate or appear to violate the law. It uses import alerts to place products under detention without physical examination. In September 2025, FDA created a GLP 1 API import action aimed at unverified foreign sources. [(FDA Import Basics)](https://www.fda.gov/industry/import-program/import-basics) [(FDA Import Alerts)](https://www.fda.gov/industry/actions-enforcement/import-alerts) [(FDA green list GLP-1 release)](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-launches-green-list-protect-americans-illegal-imported-glp-1-drug-ingredients)

State law fills in the rest. State consumer protection laws let attorneys general challenge false or misleading marketing even when FDA has not yet acted. That is what Connecticut did against Triggered Brand and Made in China, and what Ohio did when it warned 14 med spas over compounded GLP 1 claims. State pharmacy and professional licensing laws also matter because they control who may dispense, prescribe, compound, or distribute prescription drugs. Washington used pharmacy enforcement power against Aequita Pharmacy over sterile compounding failures. [(Connecticut AG release)](https://portal.ct.gov/ag/press-releases/2025-press-releases/attorney-general-tong-sues-glp-1-weight-loss-drug-distributor-triggered-brand) [(Ohio AG release)](https://www.ohioattorneygeneral.gov/Media/News-Releases/April-2025/AG-Yost-Warns-Med-Spas-Stop-Misleading-Consumers-A) [(Washington Aequita action)](https://doh.wa.gov/newsroom/pharmacy-quality-assurance-commission-issues-limited-stop-service-license-pharmacy)

Mail fraud and wire fraud are not FDA statutes. They are general federal fraud crimes. The statutes cover schemes to defraud carried out through the mail or interstate wires such as websites, texts, calls, email, online payments, and shipping. In this market, those theories become more likely when a seller lies about what the product is, where it came from, whether it is FDA approved, who made it, or whether a patient needs a prescription. The Reyes and Watkins cases show how federal prosecutors move beyond labeling violations when they see deliberate deception, relabeling, patient harm risk, or direct sales to patients. This is an interpretation based on the criminal statutes and the charging patterns in those cases. [(18 USC chapter 63)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-chapter63&saved=%7CKHRpdGxlOjE4IFVTQyBzZWN0aW9uOjEzNDEgZWRpdGlvbjpwcmVsaW0pIE9SIChncmFudWxlaWQ6VVNDLXByZWxpbS10aXRsZTE4IFVTQy1zZWN0aW9uMTM0MSk%3D%7CdHJlZXNvcnQ%3D%7C%7C0%7Cfalse%7Cprelim) [(DOJ Reyes case)](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/long-island-woman-arrested-selling-misbranded-and-adulterated-weight-loss-drugs) [(DOJ Watkins case)](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ut/pr/utah-licensed-osteopathic-physician-indicted-allegedly-receiving-misbranded-drugs-china)

So what would an actually lawful research supplier look like. At a high level, it would stay inside genuine laboratory or institutional research channels, avoid claims about weight loss or treatment, avoid consumer style branding that implies self use, avoid selling injection prep supplies beside the peptide, and avoid any conduct that suggests the seller expects home use. If the business is really in human drug compounding or manufacturing, it would also need to satisfy the compounding or drug manufacturing rules that apply to that activity. That is very different from the grey market pattern documented in the warning letters, where sellers often target ordinary consumers while using thin legal disclaimers. [(21 CFR 201.128)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title21-vol4/pdf/CFR-2025-title21-vol4-sec201-128.pdf) [(FDA concerns with unapproved GLP-1 drugs)](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/postmarket-drug-safety-information-patients-and-providers/medications-containing-semaglutide-marketed-type-2-diabetes-or-weight-loss?os=av) [(FDA bulk substances page)](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/bulk-drug-substances-used-compounding)

| FD&C Act section 505(a) | New drugs generally cannot be sold in the US without an FDA approved application. | [Section 503A page quoting 505 exemption](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/section-503a-federal-food-drug-and-cosmetic-act) |

| FD&C Act section 502(bb) | A compounded drug is misbranded if its advertising or promotion is false or misleading. | [BluefitMD warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/bluefitmd-721446-02202026) |

| FD&C Act section 801 and FDA import alert system | FDA can detain or refuse imported products that appear to violate the law, including adulterated or misbranded drugs and APIs. | [FDA Import Alerts](https://www.fda.gov/industry/actions-enforcement/import-alerts), [FDA Import Basics](https://www.fda.gov/industry/import-program/import-basics) |
| 18 USC 1341 and 1343 | Mail fraud and wire fraud can apply when sellers use shipping, internet, texts, calls, or payments as part of a deliberate deception scheme. | [18 USC chapter 63](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-chapter63&saved=%7CKHRpdGxlOjE4IFVTQyBzZWN0aW9uOjEzNDEgZWRpdGlvbjpwcmVsaW0pIE9SIChncmFudWxlaWQ6VVNDLXByZWxpbS10aXRsZTE4IFVTQy1zZWN0aW9uMTM0MSk%3D%7CdHJlZXNvcnQ%3D%7C%7C0%7Cfalse%7Cprelim) |

## C. Table of common disclaimers and why each one fails

| Disclaimer or tactic | What it tries to do | Why it often fails | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Research use only | Pretend the seller is serving labs, not people | FDA still looks at claims, design, and distribution circumstances | [21 CFR 201.128](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title21-vol4/pdf/CFR-2025-title21-vol4-sec201-128.pdf), [US Chem Labs](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/us-chem-labs-669074-02072024) |
| Not for human consumption | Same basic shield as above | It does not erase weight loss, disease, or injection signals elsewhere on the site | [Helix](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/synthetix-inc-dba-helix-chemical-supply-668918-02072024), [Gram Peptides](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/gram-peptides-721806-03312026) |
| Not FDA approved | Admit there is no approval while still chasing consumer sales | This may avoid one false claim, but it does not solve unapproved new drug, misbranding, or intended use problems | [FDA concerns with unapproved GLP-1 drugs](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/postmarket-drug-safety-information-patients-and-providers/medications-containing-semaglutide-marketed-type-2-diabetes-or-weight-loss?os=av) |
| Same active ingredient as Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, or Zepbound | Borrow trust from approved drugs | FDA says this can falsely imply approval, sameness, or evaluated equivalence for compounded products | [BluefitMD](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/bluefitmd-721446-02202026), [NewSelf](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/newself-limited-dba-newself-721472-02202026) |
| For laboratory research only | Same as research use only | It fails when the surrounding marketing still points to home injection or consumer weight loss use | [USApeptide](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/usapeptidecom-696885-02262025) |
| No medical advice | Try to cast the seller as neutral | It does not cancel disease claims, dosing language, or marketing that invites consumers to self treat | [FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance) |
| Terms of service and liability waiver | Shift risk to the buyer | Private contract language does not override FDA or FTC law | [FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance) |
| Age gate or checkbox | Create a paper trail that the buyer accepted the risk | FDA still looks at what the seller is actually promoting and selling | [21 CFR 201.128](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title21-vol4/pdf/CFR-2025-title21-vol4-sec201-128.pdf) |
| FAQ pages about approved drug uses | Look educational rather than promotional | FDA has already used FAQ style claims as evidence of misleading GLP 1 marketing | [NewSelf](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/newself-limited-dba-newself-721472-02202026) |
| COA and lab tested claims | Suggest safety and quality | A purity claim does not equal FDA approval, sterility, or lawful sale for human use | [FDA concerns with unapproved GLP-1 drugs](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/postmarket-drug-safety-information-patients-and-providers/medications-containing-semaglutide-marketed-type-2-diabetes-or-weight-loss?os=av) |
| Bundling bacteriostatic water or similar supplies | Claim the peptide is only for research while selling what is needed to inject it | FDA has used bundled injection supplies as evidence of intended human use | [Gram Peptides](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/gram-peptides-721806-03312026), [New Life Pharma](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/new-life-pharma-llc-725661-04142026) |
