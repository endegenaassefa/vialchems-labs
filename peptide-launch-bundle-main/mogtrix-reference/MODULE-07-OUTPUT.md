# MODULE 7: INFLUENCER MARKETING AND CUSTOMER PIPELINE

## A. Narrative explanation of the customer pipeline

The official record shows that social and influencer style marketing is not peripheral to this market. It is part of the evidence trail.

FDA’s December 10, 2024 warning letter to Prime Peptides is the cleanest example. FDA did not only review the company’s website. It also reviewed Prime’s Facebook, Instagram, and YouTube pages and treated those channels as intended use evidence. That matters because it shows sellers cannot keep the main website "clean" while using social media to carry the human use message. [(Prime Peptides warning letter)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/prime-vitality-inc-dba-prime-peptides-695156-12102024)

DOJ’s case against Isis Navarro Reyes shows an even more direct social pipeline. Prosecutors said she sold misbranded and adulterated weight loss drugs through TikTok and encrypted messaging. That is not just a marketing story. It is a criminal distribution story that began on social platforms. [(DOJ Reyes case)](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/long-island-woman-arrested-selling-misbranded-and-adulterated-weight-loss-drugs)

Connecticut’s May 21, 2025 action against Triggered Brand says the company nominally claimed to sell lab supplies while its social media and website explicitly targeted consumers looking for GLP 1 weight loss injections. That is the basic pattern in one sentence. The label says lab supply. The funnel points to home use. [(Connecticut AG release)](https://portal.ct.gov/ag/press-releases/2025-press-releases/attorney-general-tong-sues-glp-1-weight-loss-drug-distributor-triggered-brand)

FTC’s endorsement and influencer guidance explains why this matters legally. The agency says endorsements in social media are still advertising, material relationships must be disclosed, and both advertisers and endorsers can be liable for false or unsubstantiated claims. FTC’s NextMed case adds the testimonial layer by showing enforcement over fake or manipulated reviews, hidden pricing, and exaggerated weight loss claims. [(FTC Endorsement Guides FAQ)](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides) [(FTC Disclosures 101 for influencers)](https://www.ftc.gov/influencers) [(FTC NextMed release)](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/07/ftc-takes-action-against-telemedicine-firm-nextmed-over-charges-it-used-misleading-prices-fake)

In practice, the customer pipeline often seems to work like this.

First, the buyer encounters peptide or GLP 1 content through social media, online reviews, search, or community chatter. In the official record, TikTok, Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, and manipulated online reviews are already documented. Reddit, Telegram, Discord, and bodybuilding forums are best treated as **context only** in this memo unless a specific claim from them is preserved and verified elsewhere. [(Prime Peptides warning letter)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/prime-vitality-inc-dba-prime-peptides-695156-12102024) [(DOJ Reyes case)](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/long-island-woman-arrested-selling-misbranded-and-adulterated-weight-loss-drugs) [(FTC NextMed release)](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/07/ftc-takes-action-against-telemedicine-firm-nextmed-over-charges-it-used-misleading-prices-fake)

Second, the buyer lands on a brand site built to look technical, measured, or clinically adjacent. Prime Peptides offers testing pages and COAs. Gram Peptides markets itself as pharmacist formulated and third party tested. That gives the customer a bridge from hype into apparent legitimacy. [(Prime Peptides testing page)](https://primepeptides.co/testing/) [(Gram home page)](https://grampeptides.com/)

Third, the product page turns the curiosity into intent. The page may still say research only, but it often describes appetite control, glucose balance, metabolic regulation, recovery, or clinic directed subcutaneous use. Prime’s retatrutide page and Gram’s cagrilintide page show exactly that pattern. [(Prime Retatrutide product page)](https://primepeptides.co/products/reta) [(Gram cagrilintide page)](https://grampeptides.com/product/cagrilintide/)

Fourth, the site offers checkout, testing proof, and sometimes the supplies needed to use the product. FDA has specifically treated bundled bacteriostatic water and syringes as intended use evidence in USApeptide, Gram Peptides, and New Life Pharma. [(USApeptide warning letter)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/usapeptidecom-696885-02262025) [(Gram Peptides warning letter)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/gram-peptides-721806-03312026) [(New Life Pharma warning letter)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/new-life-pharma-llc-725661-04142026)

Fifth, in telehealth versions of the pipeline, the buyer moves into a medicalized flow with online intake, telehealth consent, and mail order pharmacy language. Villas Health’s consent page is a public example of that layer. [(Villas Health consent page)](https://villashealth.com/consent-to-telehealth/)

The biggest reporting lesson is that **affiliate and influencer content can carry the human use claims that the main storefront wants to soften**. FTC’s guidance says a company is still responsible for endorsement claims and for undisclosed material connections. FDA’s Prime Peptides letter shows social channels can be pulled directly into the intended use case. That makes screenshots, affiliate codes, and archived social posts high value evidence. [(FTC Endorsement Guides FAQ)](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides) [(FTC Disclosures 101 for influencers)](https://www.ftc.gov/influencers) [(Prime Peptides warning letter)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/prime-vitality-inc-dba-prime-peptides-695156-12102024)

## B. Customer journey step by step description

1. Customer sees a social post, review, affiliate mention, or online discussion about a peptide or GLP 1 product.
2. Customer clicks to a branded site that emphasizes testing, purity, secure payment, or clinical sounding presentation.
3. Customer lands on a product page that mixes research only disclaimers with metabolism, recovery, appetite, or injection adjacent language.
4. Customer is shown COAs, testing pages, or scientific blog content that lowers skepticism.
5. Customer adds the vial to cart and may also see bacteriostatic water, syringes, or other handling supplies.
6. Customer accepts site terms, checkout terms, or telehealth consent.
7. Customer receives shipment and may then rely on on site instructions, outside influencer content, or community posts to decide how to use the product. The last point is an **INVESTIGATIVE HYPOTHESIS** unless documented through preserved emails, inserts, or direct evidence.

## C. List of evidence a journalist should collect about marketing

1. Archived social media posts from the brand and named affiliates
2. Screenshots of coupon codes, referral links, or affiliate disclosures
3. Product pages that mix disclaimers with human use themes
4. COA and testing pages used as trust signals
5. Review widgets, before and after photos, and testimonial pages
6. Telehealth intake, consent, and pricing pages
7. Search snippets, page titles, and metadata built around GLP 1 demand terms
8. Emails, SMS messages, or private group invites sent after checkout
9. Video clips or transcripts from podcasts or creator promotions, if preserved
10. FTC complaint material or warning letters showing how regulators interpreted the marketing
