# MODULE 8: CONSUMER RISK AND PRODUCT SAFETY

## A. Narrative explanation of consumer risks

Consumers buying from grey market peptide sites face at least four separate types of risk: product quality risk, dosing risk, medical risk, and fraud risk.

The first risk is **quality**. FDA repeatedly says compounded drugs are not FDA approved and are not reviewed by the agency for safety, effectiveness, or quality before marketing. That is not a technical footnote. It means buyers cannot assume the basic controls that exist in the approved drug supply chain. [(FDA risks of compounded drugs)](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/understanding-risks-compounded-drugs)

The second risk is **contamination or sterility failure**, especially with injections. FDA’s November 1, 2024 alert on Fullerton Wellness is the strongest concrete example in this project. FDA said the company used non sterile ingredients to make injectable semaglutide and tirzepatide and took no sterilization step. The agency warned that administration of a non sterile drug intended to be sterile may lead to serious and life threatening consequences including infections and sepsis. [(Fullerton Wellness alert)](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fda-warns-patients-and-health-care-professionals-not-use-compounded-drugs-fullerton-wellness)

The third risk is **wrong dose or wrong concentration**. FDA’s compounding alert on injectable semaglutide says patients and providers made dosing errors because compounded products came in different concentrations and some instructions used "units" rather than milligrams or milliliters. Reported overdose events included nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, fainting, dehydration, acute pancreatitis, and gallstones. A published case series from 2024 also described unintentional semaglutide overdoses during initiation. [(FDA semaglutide dosing alert)](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fda-alerts-health-care-providers-compounders-and-patients-dosing-errors-associated-compounded) [(Semaglutide overdose case series, PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38470137/)

The fourth risk is **fraudulent or falsified product**. FDA’s GLP 1 concerns page says the agency is aware of fraudulent compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide products with false label information, including cases where the named compounding pharmacy did not exist or did not actually make the product. FDA also issued a counterfeit Ozempic warning in April 2025 after several hundred units entered the U.S. drug supply chain outside authorized channels. A 2025 case report described hypoglycaemic coma after exposure to a falsified semaglutide product. [(FDA concerns page)](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/postmarket-drug-safety-information-patients-and-providers/medications-containing-semaglutide-marketed-type-2-diabetes-or-weight-loss?os=av) [(FDA counterfeit Ozempic alert)](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fda-warns-consumers-not-use-counterfeit-ozempic-semaglutide-found-us-drug-supply-chain) [(Falsified semaglutide case report, PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41125326/)

The fifth risk is **poor ingredient sourcing**. FDA’s 2025 and 2026 GLP 1 actions say the agency is concerned about foreign made APIs, unapproved salt forms of semaglutide, and poor quality bulk drug substances. FDA now subjects many imported GLP 1 APIs from unverified sources to detention without physical examination. That does not prove every imported API is unsafe. It does show FDA sees supply chain quality as a major patient safety issue. [(FDA green list release)](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-launches-green-list-protect-americans-illegal-imported-glp-1-drug-ingredients) [(FDA concerns page)](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/postmarket-drug-safety-information-patients-and-providers/medications-containing-semaglutide-marketed-type-2-diabetes-or-weight-loss?os=av)

The sixth risk is **missing or misleading testing**. A COA may show identity or purity. It does not necessarily show sterility, endotoxin control, stability, fill accuracy, or authentic chain of custody from the original API manufacturer. FDA’s supplier guidance says compounders must know their suppliers and preserve transparency. FDA warning letters to BioStem and Advanced Pharmaceutical Technology show that sterility and endotoxin testing alone do not demonstrate identity or strength, and that final sterility testing alone cannot justify batch release. [(FDA to compounders: know your bulks suppliers)](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fda-compounders-know-your-bulks-and-excipients-suppliers) [(BioStem warning letter)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/biostem-life-sciences-673788-01172025) [(Advanced Pharmaceutical Technology warning letter)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/advanced-pharmaceutical-technology-692576-03142025)

The seventh risk is **ordinary drug side effects without medical supervision**. FDA approved labeling for Wegovy and Zepbound lists common gastrointestinal adverse reactions and warns about acute pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, volume depletion, and delayed gastric emptying. Those risks exist even with regulated products used correctly. Grey market use adds extra uncertainty about dose, ingredient, storage, and instructions. [(Wegovy labeling)](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2022/215256s003lbl.pdf) [(Zepbound labeling)](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/217806Orig1s020lbl.pdf)

The eighth risk is **shipping and storage failure**. FDA says it has received complaints that compounded GLP 1 products arrived warm or with inadequate ice packs and warns that this can affect the drug’s quality. That issue matters because many grey market sales are mail order by design. [(FDA concerns page)](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/postmarket-drug-safety-information-patients-and-providers/medications-containing-semaglutide-marketed-type-2-diabetes-or-weight-loss?os=av)

The best published broad dataset I found in this pass is a 2026 pharmacovigilance study using FAERS. It reported higher odds of several adverse events and product quality problems in compounded GLP 1 reports compared with non compounded products, including higher odds of contamination and compounding or manufacturing issues, and higher odds of hospitalization. FAERS data have limitations and do not prove causation, but the study is still one of the strongest public signals that compounded GLP 1 products deserve close scrutiny. [(Compounded GLP-1 FAERS study, PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40285721/)

I did **not** locate a large, transparent, peer reviewed U.S. dataset of independent lab testing results for grey market research peptide vials sold direct to consumers. That gap matters. Much of the public debate is driven by seller posted COAs, scattered anecdotes, enforcement records, and isolated cases, not by a systematic independent testing program.
