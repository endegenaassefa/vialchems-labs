# MODULE 5: WEBSITE COMPLIANCE THEATER

## A. Table of disclaimer types, what each tries to do, and why each fails

| Disclaimer or device | What it is trying to do | Why it may fail | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Research use only | Recast a consumer sale as a lab sale | FDA looks at intended use from the whole record, not one label | [21 CFR 201.128](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title21-vol4/pdf/CFR-2025-title21-vol4-sec201-128.pdf), [US Chem Labs warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/us-chem-labs-669074-02072024) |
| Not for human consumption | Create distance from self use | FDA still treats a product as a drug when the same site discusses weight loss, blood sugar, dosing, or injection | [Helix warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/synthetix-inc-dba-helix-chemical-supply-668918-02072024), [Gram Peptides warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/gram-peptides-721806-03312026) |
| Not FDA approved | Admit there is no approval | This does not cure unlawful sale of an unapproved new drug or a misleading compounded drug claim | [FDA concerns with unapproved GLP-1 drugs](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/postmarket-drug-safety-information-patients-and-providers/medications-containing-semaglutide-marketed-type-2-diabetes-or-weight-loss?os=av) |
| For laboratory research only | Same move as research use only | FDA has used website claims, product design, and bundled supplies to show real intent | [USApeptide warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/usapeptidecom-696885-02262025), [New Life Pharma warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/new-life-pharma-llc-725661-04142026) |
| Not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent disease | Borrow supplement style language | It fails when the rest of the page still points to treatment, weight loss, metabolism, glucose control, or injection | [US Chem Labs warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/us-chem-labs-669074-02072024), [Prime Peptides warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/prime-vitality-inc-dba-prime-peptides-695156-12102024) |
| Age gate or age statement | Show the company is restricting access to adults | Age checks do not change the product’s legal status or erase intended use evidence | [Prime Retatrutide product page](https://primepeptides.co/products/reta), [21 CFR 201.128](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title21-vol4/pdf/CFR-2025-title21-vol4-sec201-128.pdf) |
| Checkout checkbox or purchase acknowledgement | Shift blame to the buyer | Private acknowledgements do not override FDA or FTC law | [OnePeptideShop terms](https://onepeptideshop.com/terms-and-conditions/), [FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance) |
| Terms of service, indemnity, liability waiver | Push legal risk onto the customer | A contract clause cannot legalize false advertising or unlawful drug sales | [Xcel terms](https://www.xcelpeptides.com/terms-of-service/), [OnePeptideShop terms](https://onepeptideshop.com/terms-and-conditions/) |
| Customer responsibility language | Suggest misuse is entirely the buyer’s fault | It fails when the seller’s own site design, product lineup, or education pages point toward consumer injection use | [Gram home page](https://grampeptides.com/), [Gram Peptides warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/gram-peptides-721806-03312026) |
| No medical advice disclaimer | Try to frame the site as neutral information | FDA and FTC both look at implied claims, not just whether the site says it is giving advice | [FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance), [NewSelf warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/newself-limited-dba-newself-721472-02202026) |
| Refund policy or shipping policy disclaimers | Limit disputes after sale | Shipping language does not solve the core problem if the underlying product is unlawful or poor quality | [Xcel shipping and returns page](https://www.xcelpeptides.com/refund_returns/), [FDA concerns with unapproved GLP-1 drugs](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/postmarket-drug-safety-information-patients-and-providers/medications-containing-semaglutide-marketed-type-2-diabetes-or-weight-loss?os=av) |
| Shipping restriction notices | Signal caution and legal awareness | Restricting jurisdictions does not cure unlawful claims made to the jurisdictions where the seller does ship | [OnePeptideShop terms](https://onepeptideshop.com/terms-and-conditions/) |
| COA and lab testing claims | Use testing as a trust signal | A purity test does not prove sterility, endotoxin control, lawful sourcing, identity of the true manufacturer, or legal sale for human use | [FDA to compounders: know your bulks suppliers](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fda-compounders-know-your-bulks-and-excipients-suppliers), [BioStem warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/biostem-life-sciences-673788-01172025), [Prime Peptides testing page](https://primepeptides.co/testing/) |
| Subscription or membership terms | Turn drug access into a recurring service with buried obligations | FTC’s NextMed case shows regulators treat hidden pricing, long commitments, and manipulated testimonials as deceptive marketing | [FTC NextMed release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/07/ftc-takes-action-against-telemedicine-firm-nextmed-over-charges-it-used-misleading-prices-fake), [FTC case page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/nextmed) |

## B. Narrative walkthrough of a typical checkout consent flow

A typical peptide or telehealth checkout flow uses several layers of language that look cautious in isolation but become weak when read next to the rest of the site.

The first step is usually a **trust building landing page**. The customer sees claims about purity, third party testing, fast shipping, or secure checkout. Gram Peptides currently markets itself as "Pharmacist-Formulated Peptides," says products are "Compounded in the USA," and emphasizes third party testing. Prime Peptides places "View Test Results" and "Secured Payment" near product selection. These are not disclaimers yet. They are reassurance tools. [(Gram home page)](https://grampeptides.com/) [(Prime Retatrutide product page)](https://primepeptides.co/products/reta)

The second step is the **product page contradiction**. The same page that says research use only often also explains why a peptide matters for metabolism, appetite, glucose, recovery, cognition, or other human use themes. Prime’s retatrutide page says the compound is for laboratory research only, but it also describes its study for appetite control, energy expenditure, and glucose balance and says peptides require reconstitution with bacteriostatic water sold separately. Gram’s current cagrilintide page says the product is under investigation for once weekly subcutaneous use and describes fullness, caloric intake, and clinic supervised wellness settings. This is the core contradiction. The disclaimer says one thing. The surrounding product design and language say another. [(Prime Retatrutide product page)](https://primepeptides.co/products/reta) [(Gram cagrilintide page)](https://grampeptides.com/product/cagrilintide/)

The third step is the **testing and documentation layer**. Many sites invite the customer to click into COAs, testing pages, or blog posts about lab methods. Prime says all peptides undergo third party purity testing and that current COAs are available. Xcel runs educational content about HPLC, LC MS, and shipping stability. This can make the purchase feel scientific and controlled even though those documents do not answer the full safety and legality questions a buyer would need answered for human injection. [(Prime Peptides testing page)](https://primepeptides.co/testing/) [(Xcel testing explainer)](https://www.xcelpeptides.com/2025/11/13/research-peptide-testing-understanding-chns-hplc-and-lc-ms/)

The fourth step is the **cart and checkout legal wall**. OnePeptideShop’s terms say the customer agrees to legal terms, represents compliance with law, and authorizes payment by major card brands and PayPal. Xcel’s terms say the website is operated by Xcel Research LLC and that payments are payable to that entity. The goal appears to be papering the transaction with consent, representations, and payment authorization language. [(OnePeptideShop terms)](https://onepeptideshop.com/terms-and-conditions/) [(Xcel terms)](https://www.xcelpeptides.com/terms-of-service/)

The fifth step, in telehealth models, is the **medicalized consent layer**. Villas Health’s consent page says patients may obtain an online visit and then opt into mail order pharmacy services. That kind of flow does more than sell a vial. It shifts the business from a simple storefront into a consent driven service model that can look more legitimate to a consumer. But the FDA warning letter shows that the telehealth wrapper does not protect misleading compounded drug claims if the site implies FDA approved equivalence. [(Villas Health consent page)](https://villashealth.com/consent-to-telehealth/) [(Villas Health warning letter)](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/las-villas-health-care-llc-dba-villas-health-09092025)

The sixth step is **post purchase blame shifting**. The customer leaves the flow having clicked through research use language, age language, terms of service, shipping language, and refund language. From the site’s perspective, this creates a record that the buyer assumed the risk. From the regulator’s perspective, that record is usually much less important than the full marketing picture. FTC’s NextMed action is a good reminder that hidden terms, testimonials, and overall impression matter more than a buried disclaimer. [(FTC NextMed release)](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/07/ftc-takes-action-against-telemedicine-firm-nextmed-over-charges-it-used-misleading-prices-fake)
