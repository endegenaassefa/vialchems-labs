# MODULE 13: OPEN QUESTIONS AND NEXT STEPS

## A. Numbered list of open questions organized by category

### A. Questions that need FOIA requests

1. What companies were included in FDA’s March 3, 2026 group of 30 telehealth warning letters?  
How to answer: Request the full letter roster and any related press materials from FDA.

2. How many semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide, and peptide shipments has CBP detained or seized since January 2024?  
How to answer: File CBP FOIA requests for seizure logs and detention records.

3. Which warning letter recipients responded to FDA, and what did they promise to change?  
How to answer: Request warning letter response correspondence and any closeout records from FDA.

4. What complaints did FDA receive about named sellers before acting?  
How to answer: Seek complaint summaries, adverse event data, and investigation intake records from FDA.

### B. Questions that need interviews

5. Why did FDA shift from research peptide storefront letters in early 2024 to telehealth and compounded GLP 1 marketing waves in late 2025 and early 2026?  
How to answer: Interview FDA leadership or enforcement staff.

6. How common is it for med spas and telehealth sellers to keep operating after a warning letter by changing language, domains, or entities?  
How to answer: Interview state AG and pharmacy board officials.

7. What do payment processors actually see when these merchants start getting flagged?  
How to answer: Interview payment industry risk experts.

8. How often do patients present to clinicians after using products bought through these sites?  
How to answer: Interview endocrinologists, obesity specialists, and emergency clinicians.

### C. Questions that need public records searches

9. Which brands share registered agent addresses, phone numbers, payment pages, analytics tags, or legal boilerplate?  
How to answer: Compare business records, domains, and page source across brands.

10. Did any of the named companies dissolve, reinstate, merge, or shift to new entities after enforcement?  
How to answer: Pull annual reports, reinstatement filings, and name change histories from state registries.

11. Which domains appeared after warning letters that use the same terms pages or support contacts as older sites?  
How to answer: Use Wayback, RDAP, DNS history, and text comparison.

12. Do any public trademarks or SEC filings connect peptide brands to larger healthcare or telehealth groups?  
How to answer: Search USPTO and SEC records.

### D. Questions that need undercover or firsthand research

13. What does the full buying experience look like today for a research peptide storefront versus a telehealth GLP 1 seller?  
How to answer: Document the funnel, disclaimers, payment methods, and fulfillment path firsthand.

14. What exactly arrives in the package, including cold packs, instructions, labels, and supplies?  
How to answer: Preserve shipping materials, photos, and inserts from test purchases.

15. What post purchase communication do buyers receive by email, text, or private groups?  
How to answer: Preserve every message after a test purchase.

### E. Questions that need legal expert review

16. Where is the strongest legal line between a genuine lab supply business and an unlawfully marketed consumer peptide site?  
How to answer: Ask FDA regulatory counsel and health law academics to review current examples.

17. How significant are the recent compounding lawsuits, and do they create any real room for the industry?  
How to answer: Review pleadings and interview counsel in the cases.

18. Can a company reduce liability by shifting human use claims to influencers or affiliates?  
How to answer: Review FTC endorsement law and FDA intended use analysis with legal experts.

### F. Gaps in enforcement data

19. Which state pharmacy boards besides Washington took action tied to semaglutide or tirzepatide?  
How to answer: Search board dockets and request discipline logs by keyword.

20. Which state AGs opened peptide or GLP 1 cases but never issued a public press release?  
How to answer: Search litigation dockets and submit public records requests.

21. How many of FDA’s warning letter targets were still selling the same products six months later?  
How to answer: Combine Wayback, live site checks, and follow up letters.

### G. Gaps in supply chain evidence

22. How often are finished vials imported versus raw peptide powder or API imported for domestic handling?  
How to answer: Compare parcel evidence, labels, and customs declarations from purchases and seizures.

23. Who is actually filling, lyophilizing, or relabeling products sold by the biggest brands?  
How to answer: Match labels, contracts, inspections, and interviews.

24. How many "compounded in the USA" claims refer to real compounding versus simple repackaging or relabeling?  
How to answer: Trace facility identities and manufacturing claims against inspections and registrations.

### H. Claims that could not be verified

25. That Delaware, New Mexico, and Nevada are the dominant formation states for this niche  
How to answer: Build a larger entity sample rather than relying on common assumptions.

26. That crypto, ACH, Zelle, or Cash App are dominant payment channels across the named sellers  
How to answer: Capture checkout pages, receipts, and customer statements.

27. That most rebranding happens immediately after a warning letter  
How to answer: Build precise before and after timelines with archives and records.

28. That seller posted COAs are routinely fabricated rather than merely incomplete  
How to answer: Obtain original lab verification and chain of custody records.
