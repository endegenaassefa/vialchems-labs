# MODULE 12: EVIDENCE COLLECTION CHECKLIST

## A. Checklist organized by category

### A. Official enforcement documents

1. FDA warning letters to peptide sellers and telehealth GLP 1 marketers  
Where: FDA warning letter database  
Why: Establishes names, dates, product claims, and legal theories  
Priority: Essential

2. FDA press releases on GLP 1 imports, telehealth warning waves, and counterfeit products  
Where: FDA news and drug alerts pages  
Why: Shows market wide enforcement posture and timing  
Priority: Essential

3. FTC complaints, orders, and case pages such as NextMed  
Where: FTC case library  
Why: Documents deceptive pricing, review, and testimonial theories  
Priority: Essential

4. State AG complaint, demand letter, and warning letter files  
Where: State AG websites and public records requests  
Why: Adds local cases and concrete evidence against named companies  
Priority: Essential

5. State pharmacy board orders, consent decrees, and inspection findings  
Where: Board websites, minutes, or public records requests  
Why: Shows sterile handling and licensing failures  
Priority: Important

### B. Court filings and legal records

1. DOJ indictments, informations, plea agreements, and sentencing materials  
Where: PACER and DOJ press releases  
Why: Shows criminal conduct, patient harm risk, and supply chain detail  
Priority: Essential

2. Civil complaints and motions in compounding litigation such as Outsourcing Facilities Association v. FDA  
Where: PACER and court dockets  
Why: Shows formal industry legal defenses  
Priority: Important

3. State court filings in AG cases such as Triggered Brand  
Where: State court docket systems  
Why: Often contains exhibits beyond the press release  
Priority: Essential

### C. State business records

1. Entity formation documents  
Where: Secretary of State business searches  
Why: Confirms legal name, date, and state  
Priority: Essential

2. Annual reports and reinstatement filings  
Where: State business record systems  
Why: Shows whether a company stayed active after enforcement  
Priority: Important

3. Registered agent and principal office records  
Where: State business record systems  
Why: Helps trace shell structure and service addresses  
Priority: Essential

4. Officer, manager, and organizer records where public  
Where: State business record systems  
Why: Links seemingly separate entities  
Priority: Important

### D. Domain and web infrastructure records

1. RDAP or WHOIS records for all active and archived domains  
Where: RDAP services and domain lookups  
Why: Identifies registrar, name servers, and registration timing  
Priority: Essential

2. DNS and name server history  
Where: Security and DNS history tools  
Why: Shows technical continuity across brands  
Priority: Important

3. Source code references to analytics, checkout plugins, or review widgets  
Where: Browser page source and archived copies  
Why: Helps tie sites together operationally  
Priority: Important

### E. Archived website pages

1. Product pages before and after warning letters  
Where: Wayback Machine  
Why: Shows claim deletion, rewrites, or continued sales  
Priority: Essential

2. Terms pages, refund pages, and shipping pages over time  
Where: Wayback Machine  
Why: Shows changes in legal posture and payment methods  
Priority: Important

3. Social media linked landing pages and link in bio destinations  
Where: Wayback Machine and archived social posts  
Why: Captures funnel structure  
Priority: Important

### F. Current website screenshots

1. Home page, product page, testing page, cart, checkout, and terms page screenshots  
Where: Current live sites  
Why: Preserves the live pitch and consent flow  
Priority: Essential

2. Telehealth intake and consent screenshots  
Where: Current live sites  
Why: Shows medicalization of the sale  
Priority: Important

### G. Social media and influencer evidence

1. Brand owned Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, and X posts  
Where: Platform archives and screenshots  
Why: FDA and FTC treat these as evidence  
Priority: Essential

2. Affiliate codes, referral links, and disclosure language  
Where: Influencer pages and posts  
Why: Reveals compensation structure  
Priority: Important

3. Before and after posts, reviews, and testimonial clips  
Where: Platforms and archived copies  
Why: Documents deceptive health marketing tactics  
Priority: Important

### H. Payment and financial evidence

1. Checkout page payment options  
Where: Live sites  
Why: Identifies payment rails  
Priority: Essential

2. Customer receipts and statement descriptors  
Where: Customer interviews and documents  
Why: Shows merchant of record  
Priority: Essential

3. Refund, chargeback, or cancellation emails  
Where: Customers and complaints  
Why: Shows transaction handling and dispute risk  
Priority: Important

### I. Supply chain and import records

1. CBP seizure releases and any obtainable seizure logs  
Where: CBP newsroom and FOIA

### J. Lab testing and product quality evidence

Priority: Important

### K. Medical and scientific literature

1. FDA safety alerts and approved drug labeling
