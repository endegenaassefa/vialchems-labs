## IGF-1 LR3

Insulin-Like Growth Factor-1 Long R3 (IGF-1 LR3) is a synthetic analog of insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1). IGF-1 is a peptide hormone produced primarily in the liver. It has a role in cellular growth signaling, tissue development, and metabolic regulation.

IGF-1 LR3 was designed with structural changes that distinguish it from native IGF-1. One modification replaces glutamic acid with arginine at position three of the peptide chain. The molecule also contains an extended amino acid sequence at the N-terminus.

These alterations reduce the compound’s affinity for certain insulin-like growth factor binding proteins (IGFBPs). As a result, IGF-1 LR3 may remain active longer in experimental systems compared with naturally occurring IGF-1.

## Research Applications

IGF-1 LR3 is involved in the insulin-like growth factor signaling pathway. These studies can be looked on how IGF-related signaling influences cellular proliferation, differentiation, and survival.

Growth factor signaling used experimental models that investigate cellular repair processes and tissue remodeling. In controlled research environments, IGF-related pathways are also analyzed in connection with metabolic regulation.

Many studies may evaluate how IGF signaling participates in glucose metabolism, lipid regulation, and cellular energy balance. Analog compounds like IGF-1 LR3 may allow researchers to observe how modifications to the IGF structure may affect signaling systems.

## Mechanism of Action

IGF-1 LR3 acts as an analog of insulin-like growth factor-1.

In experimental models, the peptide may interact with the IGF-1 receptor (IGF-1R), a receptor tyrosine kinase found on many cell types.

When the receptor is activated, intracellular signaling pathways may be triggered. These include the PI3K–AKT pathway and the MAPK pathway, both of which are widely studied in relation to cellular growth signaling, metabolism, and survival.
