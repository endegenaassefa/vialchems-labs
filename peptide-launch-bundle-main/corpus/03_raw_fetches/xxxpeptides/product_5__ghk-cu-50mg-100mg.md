# GHK-Cu (Copper Tripeptide-1)

**GHK-Cu** , also known as **Copper Tripeptide-1** or **Prezatide Copper** , is a naturally occurring peptide–metal complex composed of the amino acids glycine, histidine, and lysine coordinated with a divalent copper ion (Cu2+).

This complex consists of the tripeptide **GHK (Gly-His-Lys)** chelated to copper, forming a stable coordination structure studied in cellular signaling and peptide–metal interaction research models.

**Research Use Only:** This material is supplied strictly for laboratory developmental research use. It is not intended for human or veterinary consumption.

* * *

## Scientific Classification

  * **Common Name:** GHK-Cu
  * **Other Names:** Copper Tripeptide-1; Prezatide Copper
  * **Classification:** Signal/Messenger Peptide (Research Context)
  * **Peptide Type:** Tripeptide–metal complex



* * *

## Chemical & Structural Information

  * **Peptide Sequence:** Glycyl-L-Histidyl-L-Lysine (Gly-His-Lys)
  * **Single Letter Code:** G-H-K
  * **Molecular Formula (Copper Complex):** C14H24CuN6O4
  * **Molecular Weight (GHK-Cu Complex):** ~401.9 – 404.9 g/mol
  * **Molecular Weight (Free GHK Peptide):** ~340.38 g/mol
  * **IUPAC Name:** 6-amino-2-[[2-[(2-aminoacetyl)amino]-3-(1H-imidazol-5-yl)propanoyl]amino]hexanoic acid



* * *

## Structural Composition

GHK-Cu is formed by chelation of a copper ion (Cu2+) to the tripeptide GHK. The copper ion coordinates primarily with:

  * The nitrogen atom within the imidazole ring of histidine
  * The terminal amino group of the peptide backbone



This coordination complex is studied in laboratory settings for its stability, metal-binding characteristics, and peptide-mediated signaling mechanisms.

* * *

## Research Context

In controlled research environments, GHK-Cu has been examined in studies involving:

  * Peptide–metal interaction analysis
  * Cell signaling pathway models
  * Extracellular matrix research
  * Gene expression pathway investigations
  * Redox and copper transport research



As a naturally occurring peptide complex, GHK-Cu is frequently referenced in biochemical and molecular signaling literature. All descriptions are limited strictly to laboratory and investigational research contexts.
