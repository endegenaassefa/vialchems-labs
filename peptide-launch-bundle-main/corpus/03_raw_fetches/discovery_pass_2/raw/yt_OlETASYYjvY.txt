===DESCRIPTION===
Journalist Jasmine Sun breaks down the Chinese peptides phenomenon that's turned Silicon Valley into a gray market biotech testing ground, where people are ordering experimental compounds for $50 a pop from Chinese factories and mixing them in their basements. Sun, who just published a major New York Times piece on the trend, explains how GLP-1 drugs like Ozempic opened the door for tech workers to start self-experimenting with everything from the "Wolverine stack" for muscle recovery to peptides for longevity, sleep, and cognition—complete with peptide raves in San Francisco and group chats comparing stacks at house parties.

The conversation covers the science behind peptides, the risks of unregulated biohacking, and why this DIY approach to biotech is both a product of American pharmaceutical dysfunction and a window into the broader U.S.-China technology competition. Sun also discusses her work on an upcoming AI book, why AGI timelines have faded from tech discourse, and her thesis that AI progress is "jagged"—capable of folding proteins but unable to count the Rs in strawberry.
