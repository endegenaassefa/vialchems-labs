What Are Research Peptides?

Peptides are chains of amino acids found naturally in living organisms or produced synthetically in a laboratory. They are essentially smaller versions of proteins and have become of intense interest in research settings for their therapeutic benefits and because they are useful in exploring the biological mechanisms of DNA transcription, cell cycle regulation, growth, proliferation, hormone signaling, neuron function, aging, and more. Peptides have become an essential tool in helping scientists to better understand our fundamental biology.

Most people are familiar with peptides like collagen, which is found in cosmetics, but hundreds of peptides are currently under active study for their ability to improve muscle growth, enhance wound healing, slow the aging process, stabilize the immune system, regulate DNA function, and much more. Currently, most peptides are not approved for human use and are used strictly in research settings.
