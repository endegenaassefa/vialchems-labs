# Online Advertising of Compounded Glucagon-Like Peptide-1 Receptor Agonists

**Citation:** Chetty AK, et al. "Online Advertising of Compounded Glucagon-Like Peptide-1 Receptor Agonists." *JAMA Health Forum*. 2025 Jan 17;6(1):e245018.
**PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39820389/
**PMC:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11742527/

## Methodology — verbatim

"Searches using brand and generic medication names were conducted using Google Shopping (Alphabet Inc); all websites appearing in Google's sponsored results were included"

Search window: July–September 2024.

## Headline numbers

"98 unique websites sold any GLP-1 RA, of which 79 sold compounded GLP-1 RAs or a prescription for compounded medications"

- All 79 sold compounded semaglutide
- 72.2% sold compounded tirzepatide
- 3.8% sold compounded liraglutide
- "Most websites reported certification from LegitScript"

## Critical interpretation for Google Ads channel research

This is the strongest available evidence that **paid Google search advertising for peptides DOES exist at scale — but only for compounded GLP-1 telehealth/pharmacy operators (LegitScript-certified), not for "research use only" research-peptide vendors.** The pathway: prescription drug certification + LegitScript accreditation + 503A/503B compounding pharmacy posture.

The April 7 2026 FDA enforcement action against seven research-peptide vendors marketing GLP-1 copycats as "Research Use Only" (per policycanary.io) and the September 2025 / March 2026 FDA warning letters to ~85 telehealth compounders illustrate the regulatory tightening on this channel.

## Companion paper (separate Semrush analysis)

A second peer-reviewed analysis (Glucagon-Like Peptide-1 Receptor Agonists and Pay-Per-Click Direct-to-Consumer Advertising, PMC12579337) used Semrush data on Ozempic.com paid search Apr 2022–Mar 2024 and found "$7.5 million was spent on more than 15 000 paid keywords" for semaglutide alone, generating "2.4 million paid visits."
