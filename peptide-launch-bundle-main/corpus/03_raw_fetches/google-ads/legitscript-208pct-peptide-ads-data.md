# LegitScript Problematic-Peptide Multi-Year Surge Data

URL: https://www.legitscript.com/about/press/problematic-peptides-legitscript-data-findings/
Secondary coverage: https://ppc.land/unapproved-peptides-ads-surge-208-as-marketplaces-see-276-sales-growth/ (Dec 13 2025)

## Verbatim numbers

"In 2024, LegitScript observed 308% more ads related to problematic peptides than in 2023, and 678% more compared to 2022."

(ppc.land headline cites a "208%" growth figure — a slightly different cut: "Online advertisements for problematic peptides increased 208% in 2024 compared to 2023" across "online advertisements, social media channels, and e-commerce marketplaces" combined.)

"In the past five years, e-commerce marketplaces have seen a 276% growth rate of problematic peptides-related products being sold."

"In 2024, LegitScript observed 75% more social media content related to problematic peptides sales than in 2023."

## Most-flagged compounds

"Among hundreds of unauthorized product names and terms LegitScript has observed, the most common products include Melanotan, BPC-157, TB-500, PT-141, and GLP-1."

## Methodology gap (important caveat)

LegitScript does NOT name which platforms (Google? Meta? TikTok? Bing?) the "advertisements" growth includes. The press release describes only "multi-channel monitoring of online advertisements, e-commerce listings, and social media content" without per-platform breakdown.

## "Red flags" identified

- "Not for Human Consumption" labeling that obscures intended use
- Minimal/missing buyer-qualification steps
- "Catalogs dominated by compounds frequently associated with misuse like Melanotan, BPC-157, TB-500, and AOD-9604"
- "Marketing cues resembling supplement or lifestyle-product positioning despite unapproved status"

## Interpretation

The 208–308% ad growth is a category-level signal that paid promotion of problematic peptides is increasing, but per-platform attribution (specifically: how much is Google paid search vs. Meta vs. TikTok vs. native programmatic) is not disclosed. Combined with the JAMA Chetty 2025 finding that compounded-GLP-1 vendors do appear in Google sponsored results, the most defensible reading is: paid search activity is concentrated in (a) compounded-GLP-1 telehealth (with LegitScript certification) and (b) circumvention via cloaking and pivot-page tactics by smaller vendors.
