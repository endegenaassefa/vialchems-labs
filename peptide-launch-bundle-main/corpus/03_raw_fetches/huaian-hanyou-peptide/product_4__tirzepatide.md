## Recent Research and Clinical Findings (2024–2026)

Staying current with peer‑reviewed literature is part of our **E‑E‑A‑T** commitment. Here are key recent findings:

### 🧬 SURMOUNT‑5: Superiority to Semaglutide

The Phase IIIb SURMOUNT‑5 trial, published in  _The New England Journal of Medicine_ (July 2025), compared tirzepatide head‑to‑head with semaglutide in adults with obesity without diabetes. At week 72, tirzepatide achieved **20.2% mean weight loss** compared to 13.7% for semaglutide, equating to a body weight change of approximately 22.8 kg versus 15 kg. Tirzepatide was also associated with fewer gastrointestinal side effects, possibly due to the counter‑regulatory role of GIP in modulating emetic pathways.

### 🧬 SYNERGY‑NASH: MASH Resolution Without Worsening Fibrosis

The Phase II SYNERGY‑NASH trial evaluated tirzepatide in adults with metabolic dysfunction‑associated steatohepatitis (MASH) and stage 2 or 3 fibrosis. After 52 weeks, tirzepatide resolved MASH without worsening fibrosis in **44–62%** of participants, compared to 10% with placebo. Fibrosis also improved with tirzepatide treatment, though without an apparent dose‑response effect. These findings, published in  _NEJM_ (June 8, 2024), support tirzepatide’s potential for liver research.

### 🧬 SURPASS‑CVOT: Cardiovascular Outcomes

The SURPASS‑CVOT trial, published in  _NEJM_ (December 18, 2025), assessed cardiovascular outcomes with tirzepatide versus dulaglutide in patients with type 2 diabetes and atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. Tirzepatide demonstrated non‑inferiority to dulaglutide for the primary composite outcome of cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, or stroke, while reducing MACE‑3 by 8% and all‑cause mortality by 16%.

### 🧬 Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction

Published in  _NEJM_ (November 16, 2024), the SUMMIT trial showed that tirzepatide led to a lower risk of a composite of death from cardiovascular causes or worsening heart failure compared to placebo, and improved health status in patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) and obesity.

### 🧬 Kidney Parameters

A 2025 study in the  _Journal of the American Society of Nephrology_ found that in participants with obesity/overweight with or without type 2 diabetes, tirzepatide was associated with **reduced albuminuria without adverse changes in eGFR** , suggesting renal protective effects.

### 🧬 Colorectal Cancer Research

A 2025 study in  _Cellular and Molecular Biology_ reported that tirzepatide exerts anti‑cancer effects in colorectal cancer cells by suppressing NF‑κB‑mediated inflammation, activating p53‑dependent tumor suppression, and promoting CASP8‑mediated apoptosis.

### 🧬 Safety Profile

A 2025 systematic review and meta‑analysis confirmed that it induces significant, dose‑dependent weight loss, with higher doses yielding greater reductions. While gastrointestinal side effects (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea) were common, they were generally mild to moderate and did not increase serious adverse events. The rate of serious adverse events was comparable to placebo.
