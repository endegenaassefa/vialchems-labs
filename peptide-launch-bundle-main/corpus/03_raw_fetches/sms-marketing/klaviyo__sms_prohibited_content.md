# Klaviyo SMS/MMS Prohibited Content — Verbatim relevant clauses

## Illegal or restricted substances list (verbatim)

The Illegal/Restricted Substances section enumerates:

- "CBD"
- "Vaping"
- "THC"
- "Marijuana/cannabis"
- "Prescription medication that cannot legally be sold over-the-
  counter"
- "Psychoactive plants (e.g., Kratom)"

## SHAFT subsection (verbatim relevant lines)

"Sex: Anything overtly sexual or inappropriate in nature"
"Hate: Discriminatory language, Hate speech, Promotion of violence"
"Alcohol: Alcohol includes beer, wine, rum, and other liquors"
"Firearms: Explosives, Firearm accessories, Ammo, Holsters,
Targets, Lessons"
"Tobacco: Vaping, Paraphernalia"

## Carrier-discretion catch-all (verbatim)

"Even if your use case is not listed here, you may receive a carrier
violation error if you previously experienced high opt-out rates
(more than 2%)."

## Other enumerated bans (verbatim short list)

- "Fireworks: Explosive or combustible products used for display"
- "Third-party Lead Generation: Buying, selling, or sharing of
  consumer data is strictly prohibited"
- "High-risk Financial Services: Cryptocurrency; Payday loans;
  Student loans"
- "Debt Collection/Forgiveness: Debt consolidation, Debt reduction,
  Credit repair programs"
- "Gambling: Casino apps, Gambling websites"
- "Multi-level marketing, network marketing and pyramid schemes"
- "Political advertising, Political canvassing"

## Investigator notes

The Klaviyo policy does NOT explicitly name supplements, peptides,
nutraceuticals, anabolic steroids, hormones, "research use only," or
"SARMs." The decision tree for a peptide vendor would hinge on the
"Prescription medication that cannot legally be sold over-the-
counter" clause: BPC-157, TB-500, GLP-1 analogues, MK-677, and the
other peptides actually sold by anchor vendors are NOT FDA-approved
OTC products and are NOT FDA-approved prescription drugs either —
they're being sold under the "research chemical / research use only"
umbrella, which the FDA has now repeatedly rejected as a defense
(FDA seven warning letters, March 31 2026 cohort, all targeting the
"RUO" framing for GLP-1-class peptides). Klaviyo would therefore
likely refuse SMS provisioning for a research-peptide vendor under
the prescription-medication clause, augmented by the >2% opt-out
heuristic that virtually any unfamiliar-product SMS list would
trigger.
