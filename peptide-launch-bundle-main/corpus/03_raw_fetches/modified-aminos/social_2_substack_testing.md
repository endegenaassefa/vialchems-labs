I dropped a product launch announcement recently for Flawless Compounds and Glow Aminos, but I left out something that deserves its own spotlight. So consider this the full story.

Both vendors just went live with capsule and nasal spray lineups, and they’re already on [PeptidePrice.store](https://peptideprice.store). But what I didn’t highlight — and should have — is what they’re doing on the testing side. Because it’s actually a first.

## The Testing Is What Matters Here

Flawless and Glow Aminos are now providing COAs that show **net peptide content** for their capsules and sprays. Not just identity confirmation. Not just purity of the raw powder. The actual amount of active peptide present in each capsule or each spray dose.

This is harder than it sounds. Most labs aren’t equipped to do this, especially on a delivery format like a nasal spray where you’re working with a solution rather than a straight powder. As far as I’m aware, **this is the first time net content testing has been done on nasal sprays in the research peptide space.**

Why does this matter? Because caps and sprays have always been a bit of a black box when it comes to dosing. You could have a perfectly clean raw material and still end up with inconsistent fill weights, uneven spray actuation, or degradation during formulation. Net content testing on the finished product is the only way to actually confirm what you’re getting per dose. It closes the loop.

## A Note on Spray Pricing

You may notice some of their sprays are priced higher than what you’re used to seeing. That’s intentional on their end — they went with higher total content per bottle. The Semax spray, for example, comes in at **50mg total**. That’s significantly more than the standard you’ll find across most vendors. So before you do a straight price comparison, make sure you’re looking at cost-per-mg, not just sticker price.
