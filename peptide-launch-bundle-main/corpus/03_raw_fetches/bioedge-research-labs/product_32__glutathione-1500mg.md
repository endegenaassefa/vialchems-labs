### Compound Overview

Glutathione is a tripeptide composed of glutamic acid, cysteine, and glycine, notable for its thiol functional group within the cysteine residue that contributes to its redox activity. Structurally, it is classified as a low-molecular-weight endogenous peptide with a γ-peptide linkage between the glutamyl and cysteinyl residues, which distinguishes it from linear peptides defined by α-linkages.

In laboratory research contexts, Glutathione is evaluated for its biochemical role in redox buffering, thiol–disulfide exchange, and detoxification pathway modeling in controlled in vitro and animal model systems. Analytical investigation often involves quantification of reduced and oxidized forms using chromatographic and immunochemical detection methods.
